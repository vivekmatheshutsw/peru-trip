Only one day to acclimatize

You land at about 3,400 m (11,150 ft) on the 16th and start hiking on the 17th, with the trail topping out at 4,215 m on day 2. Keep the 16th easy and plan your altitude approach ahead of time.

Bring your original passport on the trek

It's checked at the KM 82 trailhead and has to match the passport details on your Inca Trail permit. A photocopy won't get you through.

Short turnaround on the 20th → 21st

After Machu Picchu you ride the bus and train back toward Cusco and arrive late in the evening, then fly out at 8:05 AM. Book a Cusco hotel for the night of the 20th close to the airport and pack the night before.

No time change

Peru stays on UTC−5 all year. After US clocks fall back on Nov 1, Cusco and Dallas are on the same clock.

    Good to know

    Short notes for Cusco and the trail.

    Altitude

    Go slow on the 16th, hydrate, skip alcohol, eat light. Coca tea is everywhere in Cusco. If you plan to use acetazolamide, start it on your usual schedule before arrival rather than once symptoms begin.

    Weather

    November is the start of the rainy season. Expect sun, cloud and showers in the same day, warm cloud forest on days 3–4, and near-freezing nights at the high camps.

    Money

    Peru uses the sol (PEN). Carry small soles for snacks, toilets on the trail and tipping the porters and cook on the last evening. Cards work in Cusco restaurants and hotels.

    Power

    Peru runs on 220 V. Phone and laptop chargers usually handle it; check anything with a heating element. Bring a battery pack, since campsites have no outlets.

    Water

    Don't drink tap water. Alpaca supplies boiled water on the trek from the first lunch through the last breakfast, so bring bottles to refill.

    Day 4 food

    Lunch on the last day isn't included. Plan to eat in Aguas Calientes before the train.
