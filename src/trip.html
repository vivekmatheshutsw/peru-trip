Nov 16 is a full day in Cusco

After you land, check in at the Alpaca office with your original passport and pay the balance (office hours 9 AM–1 PM and 3–7 PM). Then come back at 6:00 PM for the trek briefing, where you get your duffel bags. Alpaca prefers the balance in US dollar cash in good condition; cards add 5% and PayPal adds 5.5%.

Alpaca recommends 2 days in Cusco. You have 1.

You land at about 3,400 m (11,150 ft) on the 16th and start hiking on the 17th, with the trail topping out at 4,215 m on day 2. Alpaca asked to be told in advance if you won't be in Cusco 2 days ahead, so let them know you arrive the 16th. Keep the 16th easy and plan your altitude approach ahead of time.

Bring your original passport on the trek

Permits are issued under your name and passport number, and they're checked at the KM 82 trailhead. Use the same passports you booked with. A photocopy won't get you through. To enter Peru, a passport must be valid for 6+ months past your visit. If you renew before the trip, bring the old passport too: the trail rangers only need the one that matches the permit.

Late arrival, early exit on the 20th → 21st

Alpaca brings you back to Cusco around 8:00 PM, you meet Cinthya at Casona Santa Teresa for the keys, and you fly out at 8:05 AM. Ask Alpaca how you get your stored luggage back that evening, since their office closes at 7 PM.

Permits can't be refunded

Once Alpaca buys the permits they're 100% non-refundable and non-transferable. If you ever had to cancel, Alpaca will give you an official letter for a travel insurance claim.

No time change

Peru stays on UTC−5 all year. After US clocks fall back on Nov 1, Cusco and Dallas are on the same clock.

    Good to know

    Short notes for Cusco and the trail.

    Altitude

    Go slow on the 16th, hydrate, skip alcohol, eat light. Coca tea is everywhere in Cusco. If you plan to use acetazolamide, start it on your usual schedule before arrival rather than once symptoms begin.

    Weather

    November is the start of the rainy season. Expect sun, cloud and showers in the same day, warm cloud forest on days 3–4, and near-freezing nights at the high camps.

    Money

    Peru uses the sol (PEN). Bring clean, untorn bills; damaged ones get refused. Alpaca takes its balance in US dollars. Carry small soles for snacks, toilets on the trail and tipping the porters and cook on the last evening. Cards work in Cusco restaurants and hotels.

    Power

    Peru runs on 220 V. Phone and laptop chargers usually handle it; check anything with a heating element. Bring a battery pack, since campsites have no outlets.

    Water

    Don't drink tap water. Alpaca supplies boiled water on the trek from the first lunch through the last breakfast, so bring bottles to refill.

    Day 4 food

    Lunch on the last day isn't included. Plan to eat in Aguas Calientes before the train.
