How it unfolds

Three parts, each with a different goal.

Nov 15–16 · Arrive & adapt

Get there, get logistics done, rest

An overnight flight lands you at 3,400 m with one day before the trek. That day holds three must-dos: Alpaca check-in, the balance payment and the 6 PM briefing.

Approach: do the errands early, while you still have energy, then rest. Walk slowly and drink water steadily. An early night matters more than sightseeing.
Nov 17–20 · The trail

Four days, 42 km, one big climb

Porters carry the camp and your duffel, and you carry a daypack. Day 2 is the hard one, over 4,215 m. After that the trail drops into warmer cloud forest and the days get easier.

Approach: slow, steady pace from the very first hour. Layer up and down often. Eat whenever food is offered, even if altitude dulls your appetite.
Nov 20–21 · Machu Picchu & home

Sunrise at the Sun Gate, back by night

A 3:30 AM start, the citadel tour, the Vistadome train, Cusco around 8 PM, then a 6 AM departure for the airport.

Approach: pack for the flight the night before the trek ends, so the last night is just keys, shower and sleep.

Don’t get caught out

The few things that can actually derail the trip.

Original passports, every stepAlpaca check-in and the KM 82 trailhead both check the passport your permit was issued under. Photocopies don’t count.
Re-check your bags in LimaLuggage can’t go straight through to Cusco. Collect it after immigration and check it in again, whatever your airline says.
US dollar cash in perfect conditionFor the Alpaca balance. Torn or worn bills get refused. Cards cost 5% extra.
No plastic bottles on the trailIt’s a zero-plastic site. Bring reusable bottles, already filled for the first morning.
Altitude is the wildcardAlpaca recommends 2 days in Cusco and you have 1. Go slow on the 16th and plan your altitude approach in advance.

Permits: who buys them, and what you need to do

You don’t buy permits yourselves. Alpaca buys them for you. Inca Trail permits are sold only to licensed tour operators, through Peru’s Ministry of Culture, and each one is issued to a named person and passport number.

  1. You paid the deposit. That let Alpaca request your permits (done Oct 24).
  2. Alpaca buys the permits for Nov 17 under your names and passport numbers. From then on they can’t be refunded, changed or transferred.
  3. Alpaca confirms by email. Their Nov 12 email listed both passport numbers to check, which is your confirmation. Tick “Permits bought” and “Confirmation email”.
  4. Rangers check them at KM 82 on Nov 17, against your original passports.
Your part: reply to Alpaca confirming the passport numbers are right, and carry those same original passports at check-in and on the trail. If either number is wrong, tell Alpaca right away.

    Effort by day

    How trail days work

    The rhythm is the same every day, and knowing it makes the trek easier.

    The daily rhythm

    • Wake-up: porters bring coca tea to your tent, then a basin of warm water to wash.
    • Hike the morning in small groups at your own pace. The guide sets regroup points.
    • Lunch is a hot meal, already set up when you arrive.
    • Afternoon hike, then tea and snacks at camp.
    • Dinner after dark, and most people are asleep by 8 or 9.

    Pace and the climb

    • Start slower than feels necessary. Most people struggle on day 2 because they rushed day 1.
    • Small steps on the stone stairs, a breathing rhythm, short stops standing up rather than long sit-downs.
    • Poles earn their keep downhill: days 2–3 are thousands of uneven steps down.
    • Peel layers before you overheat, and put one back on the moment you stop.

    Trail manners

    • Porters come first. When you hear them behind you, step to the mountain side of the path and let them pass.
    • Pack out everything, including tissues and wrappers.
    • Toilets are basic squat toilets at camps and a few stops. Carry toilet paper, sanitizer and a small waste bag.
    • Learn your porters’ names. The thank-you on night 3 means more for it.

    Camp life

    • Nights are cold at the first two camps, near freezing. Sleep in a dry base layer, hat and socks.
    • Keep a headlamp and water bottle inside the tent.
    • Little or no phone signal. This app works offline, so download maps and music beforehand too.
    • Wet things rarely dry in the mountains. Keep one set of clothes for camp only.

    At Machu Picchu

    • Use the restroom before you enter. There are none inside the site.
    • Daypacks over about 25 L have to be checked at the gate (3–5 soles).
    • No food or single-use plastic inside. Eat before you go in.
    • Morning cloud often covers the Sun Gate view and lifts by mid-morning. The tour usually brings the classic view back.

    Tipping the crew

    • Given on the last night, in soles, usually pooled with your whole group.
    • Typical: 80–100 soles per porter, about double for the chef.
    • The guide’s tip is up to your group.
    • Bring the soles from Cusco. There’s nowhere to get cash on the trail.

    Money

    What to carry

    • US dollars for the Alpaca balance. Bills must be clean and untorn.
    • Soles for small purchases, taxis, toilets (often 1 sol), bag storage and crew tips.
    • Cards work at most Cusco restaurants and shops. Alpaca adds 5% for cards and 5.5% for PayPal.

    Getting cash

    • BCP bank, Portal de Panes 177 on the main square: dollars or soles at the counter with a Visa card and passport (names must match). Mon–Fri 9 AM–5 PM, Sat 9 AM–noon.
    • ATMs are around the Plaza de Armas. Use ones inside banks, in daylight.
    • Banks give a lower USD-to-soles rate. Exchange houses near the plaza are usually better.

    Tipping in Cusco

    • Restaurants: around 10% if no service charge is on the bill.
    • Trek crew: see Trail → Tipping the crew.

    Altitude & health

    Altitude

    • Cusco sits at 3,400 m (11,150 ft). Expect breathlessness on stairs, a mild headache and broken sleep the first night.
    • Move slowly, drink steadily, skip alcohol on the 16th and eat light. Coca tea is everywhere.
    • Nights 2–3 on the trail are lower than Cusco, and Machu Picchu is lower still (2,430 m), so it gets easier as you go.
    • If you’re using medication for altitude, follow the plan you set before the trip.

    Sun, cold, rain

    • UV is intense at altitude, even under cloud. Wear sunscreen, lip balm and a hat every day.
    • November is the start of the rainy season: clearer mornings, afternoon showers, near-freezing nights at the high camps.
    • Days 3–4 drop into warm, humid cloud forest, so wear insect repellent there.

    Food & water

    • Don’t drink tap water or have ice from unknown sources. Bottled or filtered water only.
    • Before the trek, avoid ceviche, guinea pig and barbecue. Celebrate with them afterwards.
    • On the trail, Alpaca boils and filters all drinking water from the first lunch onwards.
    • The CDC doesn’t generally recommend yellow fever vaccine for trips limited to Cusco, Machu Picchu and the Inca Trail. Check your routine and Hep A vaccines.

    Cusco

    Getting around

    • The historic center is compact and walkable, but steep. San Blas is uphill from the plaza, so take a taxi with luggage.
    • Use taxis your host or a restaurant calls, or a ride app. Agree the fare before getting into a street taxi.
    • The airport is about 20–30 minutes from the center.

    Staying sharp

    • Pickpockets work the markets and plaza crowds. Keep your phone in a front pocket.
    • Street sellers and tour touts are persistent. A polite “no, gracias” and keep walking.
    • Peru stays on UTC−5: the same clock as Dallas in November.

    Phone & power

    • Get an eSIM or international plan before you fly. Expect little to no signal on the trail.
    • Outlets are 220 V and usually take US two-prong plugs. Phone and laptop chargers handle the voltage; hair tools may not.
    • No charging on the trail, so bring a battery pack.

    Coming home

    US customs

    • Don’t bring coca leaves, tea or candy home. Coca products are illegal to bring into the US, even as souvenirs.
    • Declare any food, plant or animal products, such as dried fruit, seeds or alpaca items, on the customs form.

    The last morning

    • Leave Casona Santa Teresa around 6 AM for the 8:05 AM flight.
    • Pack the night before. Keep passports, phone and chargers in one pocket of your carry-on.
